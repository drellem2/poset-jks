# Prior art for cyclic sieving on Cyc(P) (fake degree on T^{⊔k}, Theorem C, the G(r,1,k) version)

This note checks the claims of `notes/cyclic_sieving_cyc.md` ("[CSC]")
against the literature. Notation is that of [CSC]. In particular, X_P is
the fake-degree polynomial of Cyc(P) as a W-set, with W = ∏_i S_{k_i}
permuting the isomorphic copies of the components. F(g) = |Cyc(P)^g|.

Verdicts:
- **KNOWN**: stated in a source read here, with the exact reference and
  statement.
- **PARTLY KNOWN**: a special case, or the main mechanism, is in a source
  read here. The rest was not found.
- **NOT FOUND**: not in the sources of §0. This is the result of a search,
  not a proof of novelty.

**Bottom line.**

What a write-up should cite:
- The CSP for the rotation of the copies, and for every regular element of
  W or of ∏ G(r_i, 1, k_i), is an instance of the regular-element CSP for
  fake degrees of permutation modules: Westbury 2016 Cor. 2.6 for the long
  cycle, and Sagan's survey Thm 8.2 / [RSW] Thm 8.2 in general.
- [CSC]'s Corollary T and §4 should be presented as applications of these
  results.

What is new, as far as this search reaches:
- the identification of Cyc(P) as a W-set, and the closed form (★) in the
  numbers c_j;
- the vanishing B(ii) at roots of unity that are not regular eigenvalues;
- the criterion of Theorem C for non-regular g;
- the A_4 example.

## 1. [RSW] Thm 8.2: numbering and statement

V. Reiner, D. Stanton, D. White, *The cyclic sieving phenomenon*, J.
Combin. Theory Ser. A 108 (2004) 17–50. §8 is titled "Springer's theorem on
regular elements". Theorem 8.2 is on p. 37:

> **Theorem 8.2.** Let c be a regular element of order n in a finite
> reflection group W. Let C = ⟨c⟩, and let W′ be any subgroup of W. Let
> A^{W′} be the W′-invariant subalgebra of the coinvariant algebra A. Then
> setting X := W/W′ and X(q) = Hilb(A^{W′}; q), one obtains a triple
> (X, X(q), C) that exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon.

"Finite reflection group" means a finite subgroup of GL_N(C) generated by
(pseudo-)reflections (p. 37). "Regular" means having an eigenvector off
every reflecting hyperplane. Theorem 8.1 is Springer's Prop. 4.5, in the
Kraśkiewicz–Weyman form: A ≅ C[W] as W × C-representations. So [CSC] §4
cites the right number and the right hypotheses, and the coset-space form
is as [CSC] says. This agrees with `notes/audit_cyclic_sieving_cyc.md` §4.

**Correction to [CSC].** [CSC] says, in §4 and in its reference list, that
the number was "not checked against the paper". It is correct. Also, for an
arbitrary W-set, a more direct citation than [RSW] Thm 8.2 applied orbit by
orbit is Sagan's survey Thm 8.2 (Westbury's form, with V = C[Cyc P] and
B = Cyc(P)). With that citation, §4's regular case is a theorem, and §9 open
problem 3 asks only for an evaluation proof.

Theorem 1.6 of [RSW] (p. 20) is the Coxeter/parabolic special case. The
discussion after Lemma 8.4 (p. 39) says that in type A, regular ⇔ nearly
free.

## 2. Remarks on [CSC]

- **Corollary T is an instance of Westbury's Cor. 2.6.** The fake degree of
  [CSC] §3 coincides with Westbury's fd ∘ Z. For a single type,
  X_P = (q;q)_k · (1/k!) Σ_w F(w) / ∏_c (1 − q^{|c|}), and that is
  (q;q)_k · ps(Z(Cyc P)), because ps(p_λ) = ∏ 1/(1 − q^{λ_i}). For several
  types, X_P is the same construction for ∏ S_{k_i}.
- [CSC]'s Table verdict says the G(r,1,k) case is "COMPUTED … and CITED
  (Springer's theorem)". By §1 it can be tagged PROVEN given [RSW] Thm 8.2
  or Sagan Thm 8.2 (CITED). The audit reached the same conclusion (its F3).
- Theorem C's proof is already organised around Springer's evaluation at
  the regular powers g^{N/e} (e dividing every k_i). The part not covered
  by the literature read here is Theorem B(ii) when ζ is not a regular
  eigenvalue of W. This includes the necessity question for clause (a)
  (open problem 2).
- Not checked: whether the Lehrer–Springer eigenspace theory (for example,
  Lehrer–Taylor, *Unitary Reflection Groups*, Ch. 11, or Lehrer–Michel 2003)
  gives a general evaluation of fake degrees at non-regular ζ that would
  imply B(ii). This is the most likely place for prior art on B(ii) and
  Theorem C, and it was not read.
